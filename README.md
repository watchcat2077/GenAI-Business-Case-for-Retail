# Generative AI Business Case for Retail

A business case for bringing Generative AI into **RetailTech Innovators**, a Sydney-based online retailer that wants better
customer service and has already suffered a customer-data breach. The report weighs where GenAI creates value, what it
costs in risk, and how to adopt it responsibly.

> Team report for **CSIT205 Generative AI**, University of Wollongong (Autumn 2025).
> 📄 [Read the report (PDF, 15 pages)](report/genai-business-case-report.pdf)

## What the report covers

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Industry applications | How retailers such as Amazon and Sephora use GenAI today, plus cross-industry adoption |
| Case study | RetailTech's current systems, pain points and the impact of the earlier data breach |
| Business impact | Effects on operations, the workforce and reskilling |
| **Strategic recommendations** | The adoption roadmap (see below) |
| UN SDG alignment | Where the plan supports the Sustainable Development Goals |
| Data management, ethics and privacy | Governance, privacy law (Australian Privacy Act 1988, GDPR) and ethical risks |

## The recommendations (my section)

- **AI customer-service chatbot:** 24/7 support that uses NLP to understand customer enquiries. Sentiment analysis hands
  frustrated or complex conversations to a human agent automatically.
- **Personalised recommendations:** models that learn from purchase history and browsing patterns to suggest products,
  plus predictive analytics that anticipate what a customer will want next.
- **Security after the breach:** stronger encryption, strict access control and regular audits. The plan also adds
  AI-driven fraud detection that flags anomalous transactions in real time, and data-loss prevention, while keeping
  training data within privacy law.
